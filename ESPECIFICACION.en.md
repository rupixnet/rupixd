# Rupix specification: every rule, the attack it stops and the test that proves it

🇲🇽 Versión en español: [ESPECIFICACION.md](./ESPECIFICACION.md) (la original; ante cualquier diferencia, manda la española). This file is kept in step with it; test names are the same in both.

First version: 30 September 2026 (v0.6.1); updated the same day for v0.6.2; section 7 added 5 October. Format suggested by the external reviewer: three columns, nothing else. **Wherever the third column says "— (gap)", that is the pending work.** This table is filled from the repository's real code and tests, not from memory; every test cited exists and can be run with `go test -run <Name> ./...`.

Conventions: "rupia" = 1/100,000,000 RUPIX. "DAA" = DAA score (the effective block number). "Level" = the `Version` field of an output's `ScriptPublicKey`: 0 Gold, 1 Diamante, 2 Platino, 3 Rodio, 4 Kings.

## 1. Issuance

| Rule | Attack it stops | Test that violates it and confirms rejection |
|---|---|---|
| Genesis issues nothing (`SubsidyGenesisReward = 0`) on every network. | A premine hidden in block 0. | `TestNoPremine` (`coinbasemanager_test.go`): fails if any network puts a subsidy in genesis. |
| The block reward is `50,000,000 >> (DAA / BlocksPerHalving)` rupias: 0.5 RUPIX, halved at every halving, zero from halving 26 on (`50,000,000 >> 26 = 0`). | Changing the issuance schedule; inflation by decree. | `TestTotalSupply`: sums the schedule and demands exactly 41,999,994.96 RUPIX; documents the bound on the boundary excess (0.0031 %). |
| Every block is paid according to **its own** DAA, not the DAA of the block that merges it. That is why there is a small, bounded excess at every halving boundary (measured: 0.5 RUPIX at halving 2 on testnet). | (Not an attack: it is the fine print, published.) | `TestExcesoEnFronteraDeHalving` (`domain/consensus`, 30-Sep-2026): two siblings at the last DAA of an era, merged by a block already in the next era, both collect the old reward; the excess is exactly the reward drop, and the next block already collects the new one. |
| `MaxRupia` (42,000,000 RUPIX) is a **per-transaction** cap, not an issuance cap. | Absurd outputs that overflow arithmetic. | Inherited from Kaspa (`transactionvalidator`, `checkTransactionAmountRanges`). |
| PoW cannot be switched off on any public network (`SkipProofOfWork = false`). | A node that accepts blocks without work. | `TestSkipProofOfWork` (`params_test.go`). |
| A block whose PoW does not meet the difficulty is rejected (`ErrInvalidPoW`); a target above `PowMax` (`ErrTargetTooHigh`) or negative (`ErrNegativeTarget`) too. A properly mined block gets in. | Blocks without real work; a miner skipping the difficulty. | `TestPOW` (`blockvalidator`), on simnet and devnet since 30-Sep-2026. It had been under `t.Skip` since before v0.6.0 because it mined on every network and burned hours; mainnet and testnet are skipped explicitly (same rule, genesis difficulty unworkable in a test). |
| The algorithm is RupixHeavyHash: a rank-64 matrix generated by Rupix's own PRNG, different from Kaspa's; integer, not floating point. | A Kaspa ASIC mining Rupix; two implementations disagreeing through rounding. | `TestRupixPRNGDistintoDeXoshiro`, `TestRupixPRNGDeterminista`, `TestRupixMatrizRango64`, `TestRupixHeavyHashCambia`, `TestRankIntCoincideConFloat` (`utils/pow`). They prove the algorithm is deterministic and different; **not** that the validator rejects invalid PoW (see the previous row). |

## 2. Burn per transaction

| Rule | Attack it stops | Test that violates it and confirms rejection |
|---|---|---|
| Every non-coinbase transaction burns at least `BurnBase + BurnPerByte × bytes` (1,000 rupias + 10 per byte) in a Gold `OpReturn` output. | Moving Gold for free; scarcity not growing with use. | `TestLevelRules` → "ataque: tx sin quema muere", "ataque: quema por debajo del minimo". |
| The coinbase is exempt. | (Rule, not attack.) | `TestLevelRules` → "coinbase exenta del burn". |
| Burn outputs **do not enter** the UTXO set. | Counting what was burned as circulating; "resurrecting" a burn. | `TestQuemaNoSeGasta` (`domain/miningmanager`, 30-Sep-2026): the burn does not appear in the virtual's UTXO set (the same tx's change does); the mempool refuses to spend it and accepts spending the change; a block that spends it does not end up `UTXOValid`. Code: `mutable_utxo_diff.go` (`isBurnScript`). |

## 3. The ladder (gems)

| Rule | Attack it stops | Test that violates it and confirms rejection |
|---|---|---|
| A Diamante is born by burning exactly 10 Gold (10 RUPIX) in OpReturn, on top of the per-transaction burn. | Cheap Diamantes; a Diamante without a burn (the Gold would go to the miner). | `TestLevelRules` → "ataque: quema insuficiente para diamante (9 gold)", "ataque: diamante sin OpReturn"; valid cases "10 gold -> 1 diamante", "30 gold -> 3 diamantes". |
| A gem of level N+1 is born by consuming exactly 10 gems of level N (`BurnRatio = 10`). | Ascending with 9; two Kings from 10 Rodios; double ascent; excessive change hiding a short burn. | `TestLevelRules` → "ataque: quemar solo 9"; `TestGemAscensionAttacksRound2` → "2 kings con solo 10 rodios", "cambio excesivo esconde quema insuficiente", "doble ascenso desde rodios insuficientes", "transferir y ascender con las mismas piezas"; control "king legitimo con cambio". |
| Each level opens at its halving: level N from DAA `N × BlocksPerHalving`. Before that, the network rejects the forge (`nivel N bloqueado`). | Forging early (seen live on 28-Sep: a Platino at DAA 185,738, rejected). | `TestLevelRules` → "ataque: nivel bloqueado", "ataque: diamante antes del halving 1"; `TestGemAscensionAttacksRound2` → "king en unlock-1 (399) debe fallar", "king en unlock exacto (400) debe pasar"; **and in the mempool**: `TestMempoolRechazaNivelCerrado` (the same tx rejected before and accepted after). |
| A gem is a 1-rupia output with `Version = level`; it is neither split nor merged. | Splitting a gem; a "gem" with a non-existent level. | `TestLevelRules` → "ataque: gema fraccionada", "ataque: nivel fantasma (Version 99)". |
| A gem can only be transferred (same count, same level) or consumed in an ascent. It cannot vanish or be destroyed "by accident". | Destruction disguised as an ascent; gems that evaporate. | `TestLevelRules` → "ataque: gemas desaparecen sin ascenso", "ataque: gema a OpReturn"; valid case "transferencia de King pasa". |
| The coinbase cannot create gems. | A miner gifting itself a King. | `TestLevelRules` → "ataque: la coinbase se regala un King". |
| Corpus: 10,000 generated transactions (valid and invalid) with the expected verdict. | Combinations we would not think of by hand. | `TestLadderCorpus` (`testdata/rupix_ladder_corpus.json`). |
| Historical caps: 2,100,000 Diamantes, 210,000 Platinos, 21,000 Rodios, 2,100 Kings. They count the gems that were **born** across all history, not the ones alive: once the cap is reached, no other is born even if some are burned. A forge that would exceed them **is not accepted** (since v0.6.2; see the next row). Until v0.6.1 the whole block was rejected (`ErrGemsCapExceeded`, `ErrKingsCapExceeded`); those walls remain in the code as invariants that shout at `Error` level if they ever trip. | More gems than the law allows. | `TestKingsCountArithmetic` ("King 2,100 is born, 2,101 dies"), `TestGemsHistoryArithmetic`, `gemshistory_sanity_test.go`; **in a real block**: `TestTopeDeDiamantesEnBloqueReal` (`domain/consensus`, 30-Sep-2026): with the historical count seeded at MaxDiamante−1, the forge of Diamante 2,100,000 gets in and the block that accepts it stores count = cap; for 2,100,001 the honest builder refuses to build it, and a **hand-made** block carrying it (merkle recomputed) gets in but its forge is not accepted (v0.6.2; until v0.6.1 it was rejected on insertion), the virtual stays at the cap, an honest sibling gets in and the production miner carries on. No poison, no loss of liveness. Pending as a separate test: the case where the block arrives and is not a tip of the virtual (more tips than the maximum number of parents). |
| **How the cap is applied in a DAG (v0.6.2, consensus change):** the cap does not reject the block, it rejects the transaction. When a block's transactions are accepted (in GHOSTDAG order, against the selected parent's count), the forge that would push a gem over its cap simply **is not accepted**: the block gets in, that forge's Gold stays alive, the seal does not change, honest blocks that merge it remain UTXOValid. The mempool applies the same count at the door (`ErrGemsCapExceeded`). | **Poisoning the tip:** a block with an over-cap forge which, under the previous rule ("the block that exceeds is rejected"), took down with it every honest block that merged it or built on top. Found by the reviewer on 30-Sep. The reviewer's standing questions, all three answered by tests: what if it is not a tip? (it is merged, not accepted); what if it arrives twice? (two siblings with the last gem: exactly one is accepted, the other keeps its Gold); what if it arrives late? (the count is the selected parent's, not the arrival time's). | `TestVenenoDeTopeNoMataAlHonesto`, `TestDosHermanosUltimaGema` (two consensus instances compare acceptance), `TestForjaSobreTopeEnMempool`, `TestCabe`. The first three were red before the change and green after. |

## 4. The gems seal in the header

| Rule | Attack it stops | Test that violates it and confirms rejection |
|---|---|---|
| Every block carries in its header `GemsCommitment` = hash of the historical counts (Diamante, Platino, Rodio, Kings) after applying the block. It is under the PoW. | Lying about how many gems exist; a node that "forgets" gems. | Miner and validator compute the same thing: `TestKingsCommitmentMineroIgualValidador`; a transfer does not inflate: `TestKingsTransferenciaNoInfla`; end to end with the production miner: `TestKingsEndToEnd`. |
| A block whose seal does not match the computed one is **disqualified from the chain** (`ErrBadUTXOCommitment` in `verify_and_build_utxo.go` → `StatusDisqualifiedFromChain`): it enters as a header but the virtual never follows it, and its descendants inherit the disqualification. | A block with a false count; burying the lie under new blocks. | `TestSelloFalsoRechazado` (`domain/consensus/sello_falso_test.go`, 30-Sep-2026): the same block with a false seal is disqualified and with the correct seal is `UTXOValid`; the honest builder refuses to build on the false one. (Pending refinement: a hand-made child, valid in everything but its parent, inherits the disqualification through `resolve_block_status.go`; the test is missing.) |
| The count is persisted per block (`GemsHistoryStore`) and validated against the pruning proof. | A new node inheriting an invented count. | `gemshistory_sanity_test.go` (`pruningproofmanager`). |

## 5. Temporary checkpoints

| Rule | Attack it stops | Test that violates it and confirms rejection |
|---|---|---|
| Empty list = no effect. | (Rule.) | `TestSinCheckpointsPasaTodo`. |
| With checkpoint `{X, H}`: every block with blue score ≥ X + MergeDepth must have H in the past of one of its parents. A sibling of H is unaffected. | Rewriting history before H with more hashrate (51 %). | `TestCheckpointDAG` (honest gets in, attacker rejected right at the threshold, late sibling accepted; verified to fail if the rule is switched off), `TestCheckpointAplicaDesdeElMargen`. |
| Past `CheckpointsExpireDAAScore`, they are ignored. | (Verifiable expiry.) | `TestCheckpointCaducadoSeIgnora`. |
| The code and `CHECKPOINTS.md` say the same thing. | A checkpoint changed on the sly. | `TestCheckpointsPublicados`. |
| A node syncing from scratch validates the pruning-point list against the checkpoints: before importing the pruning point, every active checkpoint below it must be in the received list or be a known ancestor (`checkCheckpointsEnPruningPoints`, v0.6.2). | A hostile peer serving a different pruning point with its own proof. | `TestNodoNuevoContraPeerHostil`: five configurations over the real sync (no checkpoints; checkpoint = real pruning point; checkpoint = ancestor that is not a pruning point; **hostile peer → `ErrCheckpointMismatch`**; checkpoint above the pruning point). Red before the change, green after. |

## 6. Network and sync (inherited from Kaspa, not rewritten)

GHOSTDAG, DAA window, difficulty, pruning and pruning proof, merge depth, coinbase maturity, transaction signing: inherited from kaspad with their tests (`go test ./...` green on Linux and macOS since 30-Sep-2026). Rupix did not modify them except as listed above. Rupix's changes can be seen with `git log --oneline` and `CHANGELOG.md`.

## 7. The 51 % attack: what it can and cannot do

No consensus rule stops someone who holds more than half the hashrate; what exists are limits on *what* they can do with it and *how far*. This table tells the whole truth, including what is not protected.

| Claim | Why | Test |
|---|---|---|
| **Cannot create coins** or change issuance: every block collects exactly what its DAA entitles it to, and every node checks it (section 1). | Hashrate decides the order of blocks, not the rules each node applies. A block with an oversized coinbase is invalid on every honest node, whoever mines it. | Those of section 1 (`TestNoPremine`, `TestTotalSupply`, `TestExcesoEnFronteraDeHalving`). |
| **Cannot forge without burning, exceed a cap or fake the seal** (sections 3 and 4). | Same reason: they are validity rules, not ordering rules. | Those of sections 3 and 4. |
| **Cannot rewrite anything before a checkpoint in force** `{X, H}`, nor fool a new node with a different pruning point (section 5). | A checkpoint is a validity rule with a written expiry. | `TestCheckpointDAG`, `TestNodoNuevoContraPeerHostil`. |
| **Cannot reorganize more than `MergeDepth` = 3,600 blocks (~1 h)** back: a block whose chain forks below the merge depth is rejected with `ErrViolatingBoundedMergeDepth`. | Inherited from Kaspa. | `TestBoundedMergeDepth` (`finality_test.go`). |
| **Cannot move an already-synced node onto a chain that does not contain its finality point** (`FinalityDuration` = 24 h): the node does not switch its virtual to that chain and writes `Finality Violation Detected` to the log. | Inherited from Kaspa. This protects **already-synced** nodes; a node starting from scratch has no finality point, which is why the checkpoints and the v0.6.2 check exist. | `TestFinality`, `TestFinalityResolveVirtual`. |
| **Can reorganize the last hour** (fewer than 3,600 blocks) and thereby **undo a recent transaction** (double spend). | That is the definition of the attack and it has no defense in code. The defense is **waiting**: with less than 50 % of the hashrate, every block on top makes the attack exponentially more expensive; with more than 50 % sustained, nothing under 3,600 blocks is final. We do not give a number of "safe confirmations" because it depends on how much external hashrate there is, and that is measured, not promised (criterion N3 of `MAINNET.md`). | (No test because there is no rule; it is here so nobody believes there is one.) |
| **Can censor**: leave transactions out of its blocks and, if it never points to other people's blocks, the transactions only those blocks carry are not accepted while it lasts. | In a DAG the minority's blocks are not lost, but acceptance is decided by the selected-chain block that merges them; if the selected chain is the attacker's and it does not merge them, there is no acceptance. | (No rule possible; inherent to PoW.) |

What really changes the cost of the attack is not in this file: it is how much hashrate that is not ours exists (N3), how many external nodes verify (N1, N2) and, at the start, the temporary checkpoints Bitcoin also used (`MAINNET.md`, section 1b). An attacker who knocks down the last hour of a network where nobody trusts fewer than an hour of confirmations gains nothing; that is the stance we will give in the merchants' guide when there is one.

## The gaps, in order of importance

0. ~~Invalid PoW rejected, in the suite.~~ Closed 30-Sep-2026: `TestPOW` runs on simnet and devnet (see section 1).
1. ~~False seal rejected, automated.~~ Closed 30-Sep-2026 with `TestSelloFalsoRechazado` (see section 4).
2. ~~New node against a hostile peer.~~ Closed 30-Sep-2026 (v0.6.2) with `TestNodoNuevoContraPeerHostil` (see section 5).
3. ~~Caps in a consensus block.~~ Closed 30-Sep-2026 with `TestTopeDeDiamantesEnBloqueReal` (see section 3): instead of forging millions of gems or turning the caps into parameters, the parent's historical count is seeded and real blocks are built on top, including one hand-made by an attacker.
4. ~~Halving-boundary excess.~~ Closed 30-Sep-2026 with `TestExcesoEnFronteraDeHalving` (see section 1).
5. ~~Spending a burn.~~ Closed 30-Sep-2026 with `TestQuemaNoSeGasta` (see section 2).
6. **Fuzzing** (since 2-Oct-2026; runs every night on the seed with `tools/fuzz-noche.sh <time>`, which keeps a log and saves any failing case under the package's `testdata/fuzz/`, to be committed as is). Six targets with native `go test -fuzz` and their invariants written in each test:
   - `FuzzCabe` (`utils/topes`): if it says it fits, no level ends up over its cap; if it does not fit, the count is untouched; matches this table's arithmetic; deterministic.
   - `FuzzCalculateGemsCommitment` (`utils/gemscommitment`): same history → same seal; changing one count by 1 changes the seal; single source for Kings.
   - `FuzzGenerateMatrix` and `FuzzComputeRank` (`utils/pow`): RupixHeavyHash never panics, rank 64 on both the integer and the float path, deterministic; arbitrary nibble matrices with rank in 0..64 and both paths equal.
   - `FuzzMsgPruningPoints` (`protowire`): arbitrary network bytes never panic; an accepted message survives the round trip. **It found a real bug the first night (3-Oct, at 31 minutes; again at 27 seconds on the rerun):** a 9-byte message panicked the `GetFeeEstimateResponse` converter (inherited); with the receive loop converting every message and the only `recover` shutting the process down, a peer could take a node down. Fixed at the root and with a safety net in the receive loop (v0.6.3); the case lives in `testdata/fuzz/FuzzMsgPruningPoints/80ec04c90cc2259b` and that target's hour counter restarted at the fix.
   - `FuzzCheckLevelRules` (`transactionvalidator`, 5-Oct): generated transactions with any combination of inputs and outputs per level, burn, amount, DAA, coinbase, tomb and non-existent level, compared against an **independently written oracle** derived from section 3 (`oraculoEscalera`, sharing no code with `checkLevelRules`); invariants: never panics, same verdict as the oracle, in every valid tx no gem disappears except exactly 10 for each gem born one level up and none is born without that burn, deterministic.

   **Accumulated failure-free hours per target (MAINNET.md criterion C2: ≥ 24 h), seed with 2 cores and the miner running:** `FuzzCabe` 6 h · `FuzzCalculateGemsCommitment` 6 h · `FuzzGenerateMatrix` 6 h · `FuzzComputeRank` 6 h · `FuzzMsgPruningPoints` 4 h since the fix · `FuzzCheckLevelRules` 2 h. Nights: #3 (2→3-Oct, the finding), #4 (5→6-Oct, 5 × 2 h, 0 failures), #5 (7-Oct, 6 × 2 h, 0 failures, 1,014 M executions in total). It is met with nights alone; recorded here every morning.
7. ~~Poisoning the tip with an over-cap forge.~~ Reviewer's finding on 30-Sep-2026; closed the same day (v0.6.2) with `TestVenenoDeTopeNoMataAlHonesto`, `TestDosHermanosUltimaGema` and `TestForjaSobreTopeEnMempool` (see section 3). It is a consensus change: the rule went from "the block that exceeds is rejected" to "the forge that exceeds is not accepted".

State at the close of 30-Sep (v0.6.2 published, `80f857d7`, reproducible binaries 4/4): seven of eight gaps (#0–#7) closed and in `main`; #6 (fuzzing) remains, with the targets in the order of value the reviewer left: `topes.Cabe` (arbitrary inputs per level; invariant: never returns `true` leaving a count over the cap), `CalculateGemsCommitment` (same history → same seal; different → different), RupixHeavyHash and `computeRank` (never panic; rank in 0..64), and the parsing of `MsgPruningPoints`. With native `go test -fuzz`, hours per target on the seed, corpus into the repo. A lesson of form the reviewer left, now a standing question for every new rule: *what if it is not a tip?* A rule that disqualifies a block because of another block's content is a poison in a DAG; the right thing is to not accept the transaction. Today no Rupix rule has that shape (the two cap walls are unreachable invariants that shout at `Error`). The criterion, in one line (from the reviewer, 30-Sep): **a block only answers for what it claims about itself.** Gems seal, UTXO commitment, accepted-merkle and coinbase are a block's claims about itself: a block that lies about itself dies. What another block says (someone else's transaction) is accepted or not accepted, and never kills the block that merges it.

When a gap is closed, it moves from this list to its row in the table, with the test's name.

*Don't trust, verify.*
